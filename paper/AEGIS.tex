\section{Introduction}
The NLBSE'27 tool competition asks for a single model that reads a C/C++ function and predicts two
labels: whether the function is associated with a weakness or vulnerability, and whether it carries a
self-admitted technical debt (SATD) annotation~\cite{nlbse2027}. Submissions are ranked by a score that
weights the mean of the two F1 scores by 0.6 and the inference runtime and GFLOPs, measured with
\texttt{torch.profiler} over the whole test split on a Colab T4, by 0.2 each. The CodeBERT~\cite{feng2020codebert}
baseline of the organizers reaches a mean F1 of \BaseMeanFone{} but consumes \BaseGflopsM{}M of the 3M-GFLOPs
budget, so its score is \BaseScoreOfficial{}.

AEGIS treats the ranking score itself as the design objective. Our contributions are:
(i)~an analysis of the task and the score: the SATD label is a keyword annotation whose evidence is
partly missing from the input, and the score implies fixed exchange rates between F1, runtime and FLOPs
that an analytic cost model predicts to within \CostModelErr{}\%;
(ii)~input compaction and length-sorted dynamic padding, which alone raise the score of the unchanged
baseline checkpoint from \BaseScoreLocal{} to \BaseSortedScore{};
(iii)~a \StudentLayers{}-layer multitask student distilled from a 12-layer UniXcoder~\cite{guo2022unixcoder}
teacher, whose depth was chosen by the score rather than by F1. It is exported as a standard
Hugging Face model that loads in the official notebook.

\section{Task, Data, and Scoring}
\label{sec:task}
\textbf{Data.} The dataset derives from MADE-WIC~\cite{mock2024madewic}, which merges OSPR (Linux, Chromium,
Firefox), Devign~\cite{zhou2019devign} and Big-Vul~\cite{fan2020bigvul}. The weakness label is the logical OR of the
Devign, Big-Vul and CWE-heuristic (W~\cite{russo2022weaksatd}) annotations. The SATD label is MAT, the
``Matches task Annotation Tags'' heuristic~\cite{guo2021mat}: a function is positive if a comment contains
TODO, FIXME, XXX or HACK. Functions are clang-formatted, split 70/10/20 with joint stratification
(\TrainN{} training and \TestN{} public test functions; 61{,}311 hidden), and only the \texttt{Function}
text may be used as input. Weakness prevalence is \VulnPrev\%, MAT prevalence \MatPrev\%.

\textbf{Audit findings.} (1) A word-boundary match of the four tags inside comments reaches MAT
precision \RuleMatP{}, recall \RuleMatR{} and F1 \RuleMatFone{} on the public test split. The misses
are functions whose tag sits in the comment \emph{above} the function: the organizers' curation script
loads this leading comment but does not release it. Roughly \MatNoTagPct\% of MAT positives therefore
cannot be recovered from the input, which caps MAT F1 near \RuleMatFone{}.
(2) Weakness correlates with length: its prevalence rises from \VulnQOne\% to \VulnQFour\% across length
quartiles. (3) \CloneTestPct\% of test functions have an identifier-abstracted clone in the training
split, whose weakness label agrees in \CloneAgreePct\% of cases. Random splits are known to inflate
vulnerability-detection scores~\cite{croft2023dataquality,ding2025primevul}.

\textbf{Scoring.} With $\bar F_1$ the mean F1, $t$ the runtime in seconds and $G$ the GFLOPs (both totals
over the split, averaged over two timed forward passes),
\begin{equation}
S = 0.6\,\bar F_1 + 0.2\max\!\Big(\tfrac{1000-t}{1000},0\Big) + 0.2\max\!\Big(\tfrac{3\cdot 10^6-G}{3\cdot 10^6},0\Big).
\end{equation}
One F1 point ($0.01$) is thus worth $0.006$ score, as much as 30\,s of runtime or 90{,}000 GFLOPs.
\texttt{torch.profiler} attributes FLOPs only to matrix multiplications, so for an encoder with $L$
layers, hidden size $d$ and feed-forward size $d_{\mathit{ff}}$,
\begin{equation}
G \approx 10^{-9}\sum_{b} B_b W_b \, L\,(8d^2 + 4 d\, d_{\mathit{ff}}),
\end{equation}
where batch $b$ has $B_b$ sequences padded to width $W_b$; fused attention is not counted. This estimate
matches every profiler measurement in this paper within \CostModelErr\%. The official notebook pads the
whole split to 512 tokens, so only \PadEff\% of processed positions are real tokens.

\section{The AEGIS Approach}
\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Input compaction.} clang-format indentation becomes long runs of whitespace tokens. We collapse
every whitespace run to a single space and use UniXcoder's tokenizer. This shortens the mean input
from \TokRaw{} (CodeBERT, raw) to \TokOurs{} tokens and the share of truncated functions from
\TruncRaw\% to \TruncOurs\% at 512 tokens. Comments are kept, since they carry the SATD tags.

\textbf{Length-sorted dynamic padding.} At inference we sort functions by length, form batches of 600
and pad each batch only to its own maximum, then restore the original order. Predictions are unchanged;
only padding work disappears. The competition allows any test-time preprocessing except sampling.

\textbf{Score-guided depth.} The student keeps the embeddings and the first $k$ transformer layers of
the encoder, consistent with the finding that early layers of code encoders suffice for many
classification tasks~\cite{grishina2023earlybird}. One shared encoder feeds the standard two-output
classification head, so the checkpoint is a plain \texttt{RobertaForSequenceClassification}. We choose
$k$ by the projected competition score on internal validation, not by F1.

\textbf{Multitask distillation.} A 12-layer UniXcoder teacher is fine-tuned on the same data. The
student, initialized from the teacher's first $k$ layers, minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sum_{t} \mathrm{BCE}(z_t, y_t)
 + \tfrac{1-\alpha}{2}\,T^2 \sum_{t} \mathrm{BCE}\big(z_t/T, \sigma(z^{\mathrm{T}}_t/T)\big),
\end{equation}
over the tasks $t\in\{\text{weakness},\text{MAT}\}$, with student logits $z_t$, teacher logits $z^{\mathrm{T}}_t$,
$\alpha=0.5$ and $T=2$~\cite{hinton2015distilling}.

\textbf{Per-task thresholds.} MAT is rare (\MatPrev\%), so a fixed 0.5 threshold is unlikely to maximize
its F1~\cite{lipton2014f1}. For each task we pick the F1-maximizing threshold on internal validation and
store it in the model configuration.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
We hold out a stratified 10\% of the official training split as internal validation (\ValN{} functions;
exact duplicates of training functions are moved to the fit side). Model selection, early stopping and
threshold tuning use only this split. The public test split is used once per reported model. All models
are trained with AdamW (learning rate $2\cdot10^{-5}$ for the teacher, $5\cdot10^{-5}$ for students),
batch size 32, linear warmup (6\%) and decay, bf16, length-grouped batches and seed 42 unless stated
otherwise. The teacher trains for 3 epochs and students for 4. Inference cost is measured with the official
measurement loop (two timed forward passes per batch under fp16 autocast and \texttt{torch.profiler}),
re-implemented and validated against the official notebook, on an RTX 5080 Laptop GPU. Runtimes on
the T4 used for the official ranking differ; GFLOPs do not depend on the hardware.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\textbf{Labels.} Both labels are partly heuristic: MAT is a keyword rule, W is a set of CWE-derived
patterns, and Big-Vul and Devign labels are known to be noisy~\cite{croft2023dataquality,ding2025primevul}.
High F1 on this benchmark measures agreement with these annotations, not the ability to find real
vulnerabilities, and AEGIS should not be used as a security tool on that basis.
\textbf{Generalization.} The split is random and stratified, and \CloneTestPct\% of test functions have a
structural clone in training, so our scores likely overestimate performance on unseen projects or on later
code. Weakness prevalence also depends strongly on length and project, which a model may exploit as shortcuts.
\textbf{Measurement.} Runtime depends on the GPU, driver and power mode; our runtimes come from a laptop GPU,
while the ranking uses a T4. GFLOPs follow the profiler's convention and omit fused attention, which our
analytic model quantifies separately. Several scoring details (number of repetitions, constants for the
larger hidden split) are ambiguous in the official material; we follow the published notebook code.
\textbf{Scope.} We explored one encoder family and a single distillation objective, and ran seed repeats
only for the final configuration and its no-distillation counterpart.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Under an efficiency-weighted ranking, the largest gains for NLBSE'27 came from reading the score: shrinking
what the model processes (whitespace normalization, a compact tokenizer, dynamic padding) and choosing depth
by the projected score. Multitask distillation then recovers most of the gap to the 12-layer teacher at a
quarter of its cost. MAT is effectively solved up to the information missing from the released functions;
remaining errors are in weakness detection, especially for short functions. Code, configurations, experiment
logs and the trained model are available at \url{https://github.com/4waiz/AEGIS}.
