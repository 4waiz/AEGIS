\begin{abstract}
The NLBSE'27 tool competition ranks single multitask models that flag weaknesses and self-admitted technical
debt (SATD) in C/C++ functions by a score that combines mean F1 with inference runtime and GFLOPs. An audit of
the data shows that the SATD label is a comment-tag rule, that the weakness label of 80\% of the functions comes
from a CWE-pattern heuristic, and that the official pipeline spends most of its computation on padding while
truncating the tags it needs. AEGIS (An Efficiency-Guided Inference System) shortens the input, batches by
length, and distills a 12-layer UniXcoder teacher into a 3-layer student whose depth is chosen by the score.
On the public test split it raises mean F1 from \BaseMeanFone{} to \FinalMeanFone{} with
\FinalGflopsRatio$\times$ fewer GFLOPs than the CodeBERT baseline. On a Colab T4, the ranking hardware, it runs in
\TfourAegisRuntime\,s instead of \TfourBaseRuntime\,s and the competition score rises from \TfourBaseScore{} to
\TfourAegisScore; run through the unmodified official notebook, it keeps a mean F1 of \VanillaMeanFone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The NLBSE'27 tool competition asks for one model that reads a C/C++ function and predicts whether it is
associated with a weakness and whether it carries a self-admitted technical debt (SATD) annotation~\cite{nlbse2027}.
The score weights mean F1 by 0.6 and inference runtime and GFLOPs, measured with \texttt{torch.profiler} over
the whole test split on a Colab T4, by 0.2 each. The CodeBERT~\cite{feng2020codebert} baseline reaches a mean F1
of \BaseMeanFone{} but uses \BaseGflopsM{}M of the 3M-GFLOPs budget (score \BaseScoreOfficial{}).

AEGIS (An Efficiency-Guided Inference System) treats the score as the design objective. Our contributions are
(i)~an audit of what the labels measure and where the baseline loses F1 and compute; (ii)~two pipeline fixes,
whitespace normalization and length-sorted batching, which alone lift the unchanged baseline checkpoint from a
score of \BaseScoreLocal{} to \BaseSortedScore{}; and (iii)~a 3-layer student, chosen by projected score and
trained with teacher-initialized multitask distillation, that also runs unmodified in the official notebook.

\textbf{Related work.} Russo et al.\ trained a multitask CodeBERT on the same MADE-WIC labels and found no
significant gain over single-task models~\cite{russo2025vulsatd}; here multitasking is a competition rule. NLBSE'26
entries distilled CodeBERT into small students with per-label thresholds~\cite{shah2026tracc,majeed2026kd}, and
EarlyBIRD showed that early encoder layers suffice for code classification~\cite{grishina2023earlybird}.
Length-sorted batching is standard~\cite{ott2019fairseq}; we apply it to the evaluation pipeline.

\section{Task, Data, and Scoring}
\label{sec:task}
\textbf{Data.} The dataset derives from MADE-WIC~\cite{mock2024madewic}: OSPR (Linux, Chromium, Firefox),
Devign~\cite{zhou2019devign} and Big-Vul~\cite{fan2020bigvul}. The weakness label is the OR of the Devign,
Big-Vul and W annotations, where W is a set of CWE-derived patterns~\cite{russo2022weaksatd}. OSPR has no original
vulnerability labels~\cite{mock2024madewic}, so for its functions, \SrcOsprShare\% of the data, the weakness label
\emph{is} the W heuristic. The SATD label is MAT~\cite{guo2021mat}: a function is positive if a comment contains
TODO, FIXME, XXX or HACK. The organizers clang-formatted the functions, removed those over 500 \texttt{cl100k}
tokens, rebalanced the joint label classes and split 70/10/20 (\TrainN{} training and \TestN{} public test
functions, which are the organizers' validation split; 61{,}311 hidden). Prevalence (weakness \VulnPrev\%, MAT
\MatPrev\%) is thus set by the curation, not by real code bases. Only the \texttt{Function} text may be used.

\textbf{Audit.} (1)~The tag rule alone reaches MAT F1 \RuleMatFoneTrain{} on the training split and \RuleMatFone{}
on the public test split (precision \RuleMatP{}, recall \RuleMatR). The misses have no tag in the released text;
the organizers' curation script\footnote{\url{https://github.com/moritzmock/NLBSE2027-setup} (curate\_dataset.py)}
reads each function's leading comment but does not write it, which most likely explains them. About
\MatNoTagPct\% of MAT positives are therefore unrecoverable. (2)~The official pipeline tokenizes raw functions with
CodeBERT and truncates \TruncRaw\% of them at 512 tokens; all \TruncMatMiss{} tagged MAT positives that the
baseline misses have their tag beyond token 512. (3)~Weakness prevalence rises from \VulnQOne\% to \VulnQFour\%
across length quartiles (a length-only threshold reaches F1 \LengthOnlyFone), and \CloneTestPct\% of test functions
have an identifier-abstracted clone in training. Random splits are known to inflate such
scores~\cite{croft2023dataquality,ding2025primevul}.

\textbf{Scoring.} With $\bar F_1$ the mean F1, $t$ the runtime in seconds and $G$ the GFLOPs (totals over the
split, averaged over two timed forward passes),
\begin{equation}
S = 0.6\,\bar F_1 + 0.2\max\!\Big(\tfrac{1000-t}{1000},0\Big) + 0.2\max\!\Big(\tfrac{3\cdot 10^6-G}{3\cdot 10^6},0\Big).
\end{equation}
One F1 point (0.01) is worth 0.006 score, as much as 30\,s or 90{,}000 GFLOPs. The profiler counts only matrix
multiplications, so an encoder with $L$ layers, width $d$ and feed-forward size $d_{\mathit{ff}}$ costs
\begin{equation}
G \approx 10^{-9}\textstyle\sum_{b} B_b W_b \, L\,(8d^2 + 4 d\, d_{\mathit{ff}})
\end{equation}
for batches of $B_b$ sequences padded to width $W_b$ (within \CostModelErr\% of every measurement here). Fused
attention is not counted (it would add \AttnBase\% for the baseline and \AttnAegis\% for AEGIS). The notebook pads
the whole split to 512 tokens, so only \PadEff\% of processed positions are real tokens.

\section{The AEGIS Approach}
\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Input compaction.} We collapse every whitespace run to one space and use UniXcoder's
tokenizer~\cite{guo2022unixcoder}. Mean input length drops from \TokRaw{} (CodeBERT, raw) to \TokOurs{} tokens and
truncation from \TruncRaw\% to \TruncOurs\%; comments, and thus the tags, are kept.
\textbf{Length-sorted batching.} Functions are sorted by length, batched by 600 and padded to each batch's
maximum; predictions are unchanged. The rules allow any test-time preprocessing except sampling.
\textbf{Score-guided depth.} The student keeps the embeddings and the first $k$ layers of the encoder with the
standard two-output head, so it is a plain \texttt{RobertaForSequenceClassification}. We choose $k$ by the
projected score on internal validation.
\textbf{Teacher-initialized multitask distillation.} A 12-layer UniXcoder teacher is fine-tuned first. The student,
initialized from the teacher's first $k$ layers, minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sum_{t} \mathrm{BCE}(z_t, y_t)
 + \tfrac{1-\alpha}{2}\,T^2 \sum_{t} \mathrm{BCE}\big(z_t/T, \sigma(z^{\mathrm{T}}_t/T)\big)
\end{equation}
over $t\in\{\text{weakness},\text{MAT}\}$ with student and teacher logits $z_t$, $z^{\mathrm{T}}_t$, $\alpha=0.5$,
$T=2$~\cite{hinton2015distilling}.
\textbf{Robustness to the evaluation procedure.} Whether the organizers run participants' inference code is not
specified. The final student is trained on a seeded 50/50 mix of raw and normalized functions, and its per-task
thresholds $\tau_t$, tuned on internal validation~\cite{lipton2014f1}, are folded into the output bias
($b_t \leftarrow b_t - \mathrm{logit}(\tau_t)$), so a 0.5 cut-off reproduces them.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
A stratified 10\% of the training split (\ValN{} functions; exact duplicates moved out) is held out as internal
validation; models are fit on the remaining 90\% and not retrained. Selection, early stopping (evaluated every
half epoch, patience 2) and threshold tuning use only internal validation, so validation scores are slightly
optimistic. Training uses AdamW (learning rate $2\cdot10^{-5}$ teacher, $5\cdot10^{-5}$ students; weight decay 0.01),
batch 32, 6\% warmup with linear decay, bf16 and seed 42; the teacher trains 3 epochs, students 4. Cost is measured
with the official loop (two timed passes per batch, fp16, \texttt{torch.profiler}), re-implemented and checked
against the official notebook, on an RTX 5080 Laptop GPU, and for the final comparison on a Colab T4 with the released notebook. Each block of
Table~\ref{tab:main} was measured in one session;
no public-test result informed any decision (an accidental early test run, documented in the repository, was
discarded). GFLOPs do not depend on the hardware; runtimes do.

\textbf{Main results.} On the T4, AEGIS scores \TfourAegisScore{} against \TfourBaseScore{} for the baseline

(\ci{\CiVulnVsBase}). Batching alone lifts the unchanged baseline to \BaseSortedScore. The 12-layer teacher is the
most accurate model but scores \TeacherTestScore. The single-line student scores slightly higher
(\SingleScore); mixed-input training costs \MixedVulnCost{} weakness F1 (\ci{\CiMixedVuln}) in exchange for

\textbf{Depth and distillation.} F1 grows with depth, but the projected score peaks at 3 layers
(Fig.~\ref{fig:depth}, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}); the optimum is flat: totalled over the twice larger hidden
split without rescaled constants, 2 layers would lead by $\approx$0.001 (\HiddenTwo{} vs.\ \HiddenThree).
All three distilled 3-layer seeds (\KdRange) exceed all three undistilled ones (\NoKdRange) on internal validation,
and the test split confirms the weakness gain (\KdTestVulnGain, \ci{\CiKdTestVuln}). A control that initializes from the teacher
without distillation reaches \TinitRange{} (\TinitN{} seeds): of the weakness gain, \InitVulnGain{} comes from the
initialization and \LossVulnGain{} (\LossShare\%) from the distillation loss. MAT is unchanged by distillation; from two layers on, every model learns the tag rule. Tuned
thresholds changed public-test mean F1 by at most \ThrTestMax{} for the students, so their benefit is robustness
to a 0.5 cut-off rather than accuracy.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
High F1 here largely means reproducing the W heuristic and the MAT tag rule. SATD written without a tag is not
learned, Big-Vul and Devign labels are noisy~\cite{croft2023dataquality,ding2025primevul}, prevalence is set by the
curation, and the random split with \CloneTestPct\% clones overestimates performance on new projects. AEGIS is not
a security tool. Our T4 numbers come from one Colab session; the organizers' T4 measured the baseline at 198.9\,s
rather than \TfourBaseRuntime\,s. Seeds vary only the student, and we
studied one encoder family and one distillation loss.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Under an efficiency-weighted ranking, reading the score paid off most: removing padding and truncation, then
choosing depth by projected score. Teacher-initialized distillation recovers about a third of the F1 gap to the
12-layer teacher at a quarter of its cost. Code, logs, configurations and the model are at
\url{https://github.com/4waiz/AEGIS} and \url{https://huggingface.co/4waiz/aegis-nlbse27}.
